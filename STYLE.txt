GARY EATS! — VISUAL AND ASSET CONTRACT
Version: 2026-10-10
Applies to gameplay, encounter art, weather, UI and every future biome.
Numeric authoring source: WORLD, GARY_SPRITES, STREET_ATLAS, BUILDINGS, KEEPERS,
SHOP_PANES, ITEMS and DETAILS in game-data.js. Do not maintain competing geometry.

1. IDENTITY AND CAMERA
Painted British cartoon, clean dark outlines, restrained texture and soft shading.
Gary is the most realistic element. Preserve his face, hair, blue patterned knit
jumper, blue quilted gilet, trousers, shoes and chest microphone with fuzzy windscreen.
Shops are flatter painted sets. Props, food and hazards sit between those treatments,
closer to the shops. Avoid photoreal props, flat UI icons in the world and pixel art.

The gameplay camera is fixed. Lanes are parallel flat bands, without a vanishing
point or distance-based shrinking. Shops are front-facing elevations with a little
roof/top visible. Match an approved in-game crop; do not invent a numeric camera
angle, lens, horizon or isometric view. Props share the shop camera and ground plane.
Gary walks away from the viewer with his head turned back toward the audience.
Cutscene portraits may use different framing; the world camera stays fixed.

2. DESIGN COORDINATES AND SCALE
All geometry below uses the 360 x 640 design canvas, not device/backing pixels.
High-DPI rendering scales the picture; it does not change game coordinates.
Road: x=105 to x=255, width=150. Three 50-unit lanes; centres 130, 180 and 230.
Gary: player reference y=540; rendered height=142; sprite bottom at y=546.
The bottom-centre of each untrimmed frame is the current draw anchor.
Shops: left x=0, right x=271; each destination box is 89 x 236.
Pavement strips: x=89..105 and x=255..271. Segment height=360; detail zone=124.
Cobbles: 48 x 48 cells using quadrants of the source textures.
Items: kebab/fish 50 square; roast 46; fork 44; mushroom 52.
These are display boxes, not the hitboxes. ITEMS.collision is independent.
Current prop height caps: lamp 72, postbox 64, bin 52, bench 100, tree 78, board 58.
Some front/back slots reduce available height. Validate the actual slot, not only a cap.
Future forest trees may use a different background scale; they must not obscure lanes.

4. FILES, CELLS AND ANCHORS
Deliver world sprites as PNG or WebP with genuine alpha transparency. No white/black
background, checkerboard painted into the image, fringe, outline halo or baked ground
shadow. Preview backgrounds may be dark; runtime alpha must still be correct.
The engine supplies ground shadows. Separate light/glow from an object's unlit body.
Do not trim different animation frames independently; use a shared untrimmed cell.
Keep feet, hips and body scale aligned, with safe padding inside every cell.
No overlapping frames, clipped extremities or pose bleeding into neighbouring cells.

Gary walk: 6 equal horizontal cells; frame width = image width / 6. Natural width
must divide by 6 exactly. Preserve aspect, baseline and anchor across all six frames.
Gary intro: 4 columns x 2 rows; 384 x 512 per cell; full image 1536 x 1024.
Street atlas: 2912 x 2688; preserve BUILDINGS/KEEPERS source rectangles on replacement.
DETAILS w/h is the aspect of ONE prop frame. A strip's full width is not frame width.
Bench uses 3 horizontal frames. Props use bottom placement and code contact shadows.
New standalone shop canvases should match 89:236, e.g. 356 x 944 at 4x. Atlas-slot
replacements must instead respect that slot's existing source dimensions and mapping.
Do not stretch a mismatched generation arbitrarily to make it fit.

Each new asset needs: stable id, exact case-sensitive filename, source/cell size,
frame count/order, design display size, anchor, ground line, layer, alpha status,
and any window/collision metadata. Specify whether it is world art or UI art.
AI prompts are design instructions, not proof of pixel accuracy. Measure the output.

5. LIGHT, RAIN AND READABILITY
Daylight is soft and from upper-left. Night uses cool ambient colour with warm amber
windows/lamps. The storm may become very dark around the road, but active hazards,
food silhouettes and the available lane must remain readable on the phone.
Do not rely on the lantern for basic survival visibility.
Never bake the night tint into every day asset: reuse skins and apply lighting in code.

Current rendering shades the completed shops/keepers in a later ambient pass. Review
that explicitly before changing the layer order. Future target order:
ground -> world/environment -> ambient shade -> ground light/spill and splashes ->
local window light/keepers -> gameplay items/actors -> restrained foreground rain ->
edge vignette -> HUD/dialogue. Where a keeper is redrawn after ambient, avoid leaving
an obvious duplicate below. Preserve clipping and deliberate glass sheen.
Weather and light cannot cover the dialogue. Avoid flashes and screen shake.

Rain can feel heavy through sound, streak density and dark scenery, without dense
opaque strokes across the lanes. Use bounded reusable streaks and splash instances.
Splashes are short, flattened ellipses on the ground. Anchor them in world space so
they scroll with the stones; falling rain uses a separate presentation coordinate model.
Approve the splash at actual size and opacity before multiplying it across the scene.

6. THE LAST ORDERS ENCOUNTER
The stranger is an elderly former chip-shop owner in a raincoat, carrying a paper
takeaway bag that stays dry. Show a quiet human silhouette; keep his face partly hidden.
Choose one sidewalk approach for the first version. Feet remain grounded. Do not apply
fake perspective scaling as he approaches in this fixed-camera world.
Use separate world/portrait art where necessary, with identical identity and costume.

The Last Orders Lantern: battered body, faded OPEN glass, restrained amber light.
The icon must read at small size without depending on the lettering. Colour and shape
must distinguish lantern charges from ordinary food pickups and hazard warnings.
Lantern light reveals optional bonus food; it does not erase hazards or guarantee safety.

7. WILDERNESS AND EXPANSION
Replace familiar scenery gradually: fewer signs and windows, more trees, vegetation
and small eyes outside the road. Keep the road/lane contract unless a separately
specified mechanic deliberately changes it. Eyes first suggest curiosity or hunger.
Preserve humour, warmth and intrigue as the world becomes stranger.
Each new biome needs its own light/colour reference, ground material and asset set,
all checked against Gary at the same design scale. Do not treat new biome as new camera.

8. VISUAL APPROVAL GATE
Use ASSET_GRID_PROMPT.txt plus actual gameplay captures. Check on light and dark alpha
backgrounds; compare daytime, night and storm composites at native play size.
Check sprite animation in motion, source bounds, baseline drift, silhouette, text,
window/keeper alignment, shadows and safe-lane contrast.
Record PASS, FAIL or UNVERIFIED for each check. No image or tool can guarantee every
future asset will fit; measured contracts and review gates are required every time.
The old ASSET_GRID.json/png is a reference snapshot and must be refreshed after changes.
